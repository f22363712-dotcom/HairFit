01 · The kernel

From your inspiration to a haircut you can feel.

The user starts with a photo of themselves and a hairstyle they already want. HairFit first shows that direction on their face, then makes the decision physical through direct three-dimensional cutting.

The sequence is the product.

A real two-image preview establishes “this is what I want to explore.” The virtual cut turns that image into an interaction whose consequence the user can see.

The “oh, that’s cool” beat

02A hand or pointer cuts the 3D rehearsal
03A clump falls and the hairstyle updates
02 · Who and why

A high-intent moment with a costly mistake.

The target user is about to make a real change and wants evidence before committing. A poor decision can affect confidence and appearance for weeks or months.

Today

They already saved a hairstyle they like, but must imagine how that reference will translate from another face to their own. Static before-and-after tools stop at one generated image.

With HairFit

They upload the selfie and reference, see the direction on their own face, then continue into a 3D rehearsal and shape the silhouette through direct interaction.

03 · The loop

From “I want this” to “I can shape it.”

The proof of concept connects the natural reference-photo moment to the unusual act of cutting a three-dimensional hairstyle.

Bring the intent

Upload a selfie and the hairstyle reference the user already has in mind.

See it

Generate a real preview that preserves the recognizable person and applies the reference direction.

Enter the sandbox

Move from the photographic target into a rotatable 3D rehearsal on a built-in head.

Cut and judge

Sweep a hand or pointer through hair, watch a clump fall, then continue shaping or reset.

04 · The proof

Working means both stages are real.

The judge must see two images produce a personal preview, then see the separate 3D cut with falling hair. Neither stage is shown as a mock screenshot.

The finish line

A user uploads a selfie and reference, receives a real AI hairstyle preview, enters the 3D sandbox, performs a gesture or pointer cut, sees a clump fall, and watches the rehearsal update. The 3D stage is clearly identified as a built-in head rather than the uploaded person.

The showcase sequence connects a personal “could this work on me?” preview to one uninterrupted 3D cut with visible physical consequence.
05 · Boundary

Keep the experiment sharp.

The proof of concept protects one signature interaction. Everything else has a named place so no direction disappears silently.

Now · the POC
  • One real two-image AI hairstyle preview from a selfie and reference image.
  • One built-in stylized 3D head for the cut rehearsal.
  • Hand gesture and pointer input in a desktop browser.
  • A visible cut, animated falling hair, and an immediately updated hairstyle.
  • Repeated cutting and a way to reset the experiment.
  • A short end-to-end screen recording.
Later · real personalization
  • Revisit personalized 3D representation only if a later technique can support reliable capture and direct hairstyle editing.
  • Washing, blow-drying, styling, and varied hair physics.
  • Automated suitability guidance.
  • A communication result for a stylist.
  • Accounts, history, and hairstyle presets.
Cut · from this POC
  • Personal 3D reconstruction from this POC because the personal stage uses a real two-image preview and the cut stage uses a built-in rehearsal head.
  • Gaussian-splatting projects such as OOOSplat, LAM, and FaceLift because they add desktop or GPU dependencies and do not provide directly editable hair strands.
  • Three service simulations, which would dilute one coherent cutting loop.
  • Automatic suitability scores, which would not prove the visual experiment.
  • Stylist export and sharing, which depend on a convincing preview.
06 · Why this learner

Product judgment meets visible evidence.

The learner will own the decisions that require judgment. The agent will own implementation, and each change will be checked through observable behavior.

HairFit matters because the learner wants to remove a costly real-world decision mistake. The project is also a rehearsal for agent collaboration: what did the learner decide, what did the agent interpret, and what did the screen actually prove?
